Building an AI-Assisted Capacity Plan for Project Teams
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Project teams often forecast capacity from a blend of calendars, spreadsheets, time entries, and instinct. The result can hide overloaded specialists, unplanned work, and delivery risk until a deadline is near. AI can help turn project plans and historical effort into a weekly view of demand, available skills, and unresolved assumptions.

It is most useful as a planning assistant that exposes tradeoffs. It should not promise that a person is available simply because their calendar looks open.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

Capacity surprises create expensive outcomes: delayed launches, rushed quality checks, burnout, or unnecessary contractors. A repeatable forecast gives managers an earlier chance to change scope, timing, or staffing.

Teams can use the view to:
- Spot work that requires scarce skills
- Compare planned effort with actual effort
- Protect time for support and internal work
- Explain delivery tradeoffs to clients early

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Combine approved project milestones with actual time data and role-based availability. Ask the model to identify dependencies, vague task descriptions, and weeks where demand exceeds a stated threshold. Require project managers to approve assumptions before the forecast is shared.

Measure forecast accuracy by role and project type, not only at the company total. When the variance is large, investigate whether the estimate, scope control, or time capture is the actual issue.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

Capacity planning is not a prediction contest. AI can make the assumptions visible sooner, giving managers time to make a deliberate tradeoff instead of discovering one at the deadline.
