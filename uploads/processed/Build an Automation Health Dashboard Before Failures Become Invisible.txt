Build an Automation Health Dashboard Before Failures Become Invisible
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Many businesses add automations one at a time: a lead is routed, an invoice is approved, a customer receives an update. The work feels finished once the workflow is live. Over time, however, credentials expire, source fields change, and exception queues quietly grow.

An automation health dashboard turns those hidden conditions into an operating view. It records whether each workflow ran, how long it took, how many records failed, and who owns the next response. AI can summarize recurring errors so the team sees patterns instead of a long list of technical logs.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

An unnoticed broken workflow creates delayed revenue, duplicate work, and poor customer experiences. A dashboard makes reliability measurable and gives managers a way to prioritize maintenance.

Useful measures include:
• Successful-run rate and failed-run count
• Age of unresolved exceptions
• Volume processed compared with the expected baseline
• Time from alert to owner acknowledgement
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Start with the five workflows that touch customers, cash, or compliance. Send a daily digest only when a threshold is crossed, and route each alert to a named owner with a short diagnostic summary. Review the dashboard monthly to retire workflows that no longer serve a business purpose.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

Automation creates value only while it is dependable. Treat workflow health like any other business operating metric, and failures become manageable work rather than expensive surprises.
