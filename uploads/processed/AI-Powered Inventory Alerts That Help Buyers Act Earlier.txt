AI-Powered Inventory Alerts That Help Buyers Act Earlier
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Inventory alerts are often too blunt to be useful. A fixed reorder point may trigger after demand has changed, while a buyer receives a long list of exceptions with little explanation of which one deserves attention first.

AI automation can combine sales velocity, open orders, supplier lead times, seasonality, and stock on hand to identify likely shortages or excess stock. It can explain the drivers behind an alert and create a proposed action, such as reviewing a purchase order or contacting a supplier.

It is decision support for buyers, not an invitation to automate every purchase.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

Stockouts can cost revenue and customer confidence. Excess inventory ties up cash and increases carrying risk. Both problems become harder to manage when teams learn about them only through end-of-month reports.

Early, ranked alerts let buyers spend attention where it can change the outcome.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Pilot on a limited product family with dependable historical data. Separate high-confidence alerts from signals with missing or unusual inputs. For each alert, show the data window, forecast assumption, potential impact, and a link to the underlying records.

Require a buyer to approve any order creation or material quantity change. Track alert precision, stockout rate, expedited freight, inventory turns, and outcomes of accepted versus rejected recommendations.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

Inventory intelligence is valuable when it explains itself. Automation that helps buyers see a risk sooner can improve working capital and service levels without taking control away from the people accountable for supply.
