Using AI to Repair CRM Account Hierarchies Before Forecasting
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Many CRM systems contain duplicate companies, outdated parent-child relationships, and contacts attached to the wrong account. These flaws look minor until a sales leader tries to forecast a strategic customer, calculate account penetration, or coordinate a renewal. AI can help surface likely relationships by comparing domains, legal names, addresses, ownership references, and historic opportunity notes.

The useful output is not an automatic merge. It is a ranked review queue that explains why two records may belong together and identifies the fields that conflict.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

When account structure is wrong, revenue reporting becomes unreliable. Reps may pursue the same company separately, customer success may miss a related renewal, and leadership may underestimate concentration risk.

An intentional repair process can:
- Give teams a more complete view of customer value
- Reduce duplicate outreach and ownership disputes
- Improve territory planning and pipeline rollups
- Make forecasts easier to trust

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Define match rules that reflect the business, not just a generic similarity score. For example, a shared email domain may be strong evidence for one market and weak evidence for a franchise network. Have AI propose parent-child links and duplicate candidates, then route each recommendation to a data steward or account owner.

Keep an audit record of the source fields, reviewer, and final action. Measure accepted suggestions, records corrected, and forecast changes caused by hierarchy cleanup. This turns a cleanup project into an ongoing operating control.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

AI can find patterns in messy CRM data quickly, but business owners must define what a customer relationship means. Use it to create a confident review queue before relying on account-level forecasts.
