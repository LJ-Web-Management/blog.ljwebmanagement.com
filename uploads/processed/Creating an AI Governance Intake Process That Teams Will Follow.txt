Creating an AI Governance Intake Process That Teams Will Follow
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Employees often discover useful AI tools before formal governance catches up. If the approval process is slow, vague, or difficult to find, teams may use tools without disclosing data flows, customer impact, or vendor terms. A policy document alone does not solve that operating problem.

An automated intake process gives teams a practical front door. A short request captures the use case, data involved, intended users, business owner, vendor, and decision being automated. The workflow then routes the request to the right reviewers based on risk, rather than making every idea wait for the same committee.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

Good governance should increase responsible adoption, not merely restrict it. Leaders need an inventory of AI uses, while teams need timely answers and clear guardrails. A consistent intake record also makes later audits, vendor reviews, and incident response far easier.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Keep the form brief and use conditional questions. Low-risk internal drafting may need simple guidance, while systems that handle personal data, make customer-facing decisions, or connect to core systems should trigger deeper review. Automation can assign owners, request missing evidence, track approval conditions, and schedule revalidation.

Publish service targets and examples of approved patterns. Track request turnaround time, repeatable use cases, missing-information rate, approved conditions, and tools discovered outside the process.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

Governance succeeds when responsible behavior is the easiest path. An automated intake workflow gives innovation a visible route forward while preserving the oversight a business needs.
