How Service Teams Can Use AI to Triage Work Orders
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
What Happened

Facilities, field service, and maintenance teams often receive work orders in inconsistent language. A short request may omit asset details, urgency, location, or the safety context needed for dispatch. AI can normalize those requests into a triage queue, while leaving urgency and safety decisions to trained personnel.

The workflow can extract the reported issue, identify the relevant asset when the data supports it, suggest a work category, and ask the requester for missing information. It can also surface similar completed orders and approved troubleshooting guidance for the dispatcher.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Why It Matters for Businesses

Better intake reduces avoidable travel, repeat visits, and time spent clarifying basic details. It can also improve service-level performance by making aging and high-impact work more visible.

Measure complete-at-first-dispatch rate, mean time to acknowledge, repeat-visit rate, backlog age, and the number of requests escalated for a human safety review.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The Practical Automation Opportunity

Begin with routine, non-emergency categories. Define explicit rules for safety language, critical assets, and incidents that must bypass automated prioritization. Give dispatchers the ability to override any suggestion and record the reason.

Use those overrides as operational feedback. They show where the intake form, asset data, or triage logic needs improvement.
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Business Takeaway

AI triage works best as a dispatcher assistant. It turns messy requests into usable information while ensuring people with the right training remain responsible for consequential calls.
