Automating Accessibility Checks in Website Publishing Workflows

What Happened

Website teams move quickly, especially when publishing campaigns, product updates, and urgent notices. Accessibility issues can enter during that pace through missing alternative text, vague link labels, poor heading order, or media without captions. Automated checks can examine a draft before publication and create a focused list of issues for the content owner or developer.

AI adds value when it helps propose descriptive alternatives and explains why a reader might struggle. It should not be treated as proof that a page is accessible, because many issues require human judgment and real user testing.

Why It Matters for Businesses

Accessible digital experiences expand the audience that can use a site and reduce costly late rework. They also improve content discipline: clearer labels, better structure, and more intentional media choices often benefit every visitor.

Embedding checks in the workflow is less disruptive than discovering a pattern of problems after a large campaign has launched.

The Practical Automation Opportunity

Place checks where publishing decisions happen:

  • Scan drafts for missing image descriptions, empty headings, unclear links, and basic structural issues.
  • Offer an AI-generated alt-text draft that a content owner must verify against the image’s purpose.
  • Block publication only for agreed high-severity issues; route the rest into an accountable review queue.
  • Report recurring issues by template, team, and content type so training can target the cause.

Pair automated checks with periodic manual audits and testing by people who use assistive technology. Track pre-publish fixes, repeat issue rate, and time spent remediating launched content.

Business Takeaway

Automation can make accessibility part of normal publishing rather than a late-stage cleanup. Treat AI suggestions as a starting point, preserve human review, and use trend data to improve the design system itself.
